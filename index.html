Australia’s visitor economy is rebuilding — but not evenly

Tourism is one of Australia's largest and most influential industries, connecting domestic travel, international aviation, hospitality, education, and regional economies into a single visitor network. Every year, millions of Australians travel interstate while international visitors arrive from major global markets across Asia, Europe, and North America. Together, these journeys generate billions in visitor spending and support hundreds of thousands of jobs nationwide. Yet the industry was transformed by the COVID-19 pandemic, which triggered the sharpest collapse in modern tourism history. While travel demand has returned strongly since borders reopened, the recovery has been uneven across states, cities, and international markets. Some destinations have surpassed pre-pandemic levels, while others continue to rely on the slow return of high-value overseas visitors.

The national picture — growth, collapse, and recovery

Before examining where visitors travel and how they spend, it is important to understand the scale of Australia's tourism industry. Domestic travel drives the majority of visitor activity, while international tourism contributes disproportionately to total spending. The charts below show how both sectors expanded before collapsing during COVID-19 and gradually recovering after borders reopened.

Section 1 — the big picture

Tourism rebounded — but the recovery remains uneven

Australia's tourism industry operates at two very different scales. Domestic tourism dominates in raw visitor numbers and overnight stays, reflecting the size of Australia's internal travel market and the importance of interstate leisure and visiting friends and relatives (VFR) travel. International tourism, however, generates significantly higher spending per visitor and plays a critical role in supporting aviation, hospitality, retail, and education sectors. The pandemic disrupted both markets simultaneously, causing an unprecedented collapse in international arrivals and a sharp decline in domestic movement. Although tourism activity has rebounded strongly since 2022, the pace of recovery differs considerably between states and international markets.

Tourism activity is concentrated along Australia's eastern seaboard

Although tourism contributes to every Australian state and territory, visitor activity is distributed unevenly across the country. Population density, international airports, and established tourism infrastructure heavily influence where travellers go and where spending is concentrated.

Section 2 — where in australia

Where tourism activity concentrates

Tourism activity in Australia is highly concentrated in the eastern states, particularly New South Wales, Victoria, and Queensland. These states combine large population centres with major international airports, established accommodation networks, and globally recognised attractions. While regional tourism remains economically important, the majority of visitor spending is concentrated within major metropolitan areas such as Sydney and Melbourne. This imbalance highlights how access, connectivity, and international aviation continue to shape Australia's tourism geography.

3
Australia's tourism economy depends heavily on international connections

Australia's international tourism industry relies on a relatively small group of overseas markets and major aviation corridors. Visitor volume alone does not determine economic importance — some markets contribute far greater spending per visitor than others.

Section 3 — international visitors

Who visits Australia — and who spends the most

Australia attracts visitors from across the world, but international tourism is dominated by a relatively small number of source markets concentrated within the Asia-Pacific region. New Zealand contributes the highest visitor volume due to geographic proximity and strong trans-Tasman connections, while markets such as China, India, the United States, and the United Kingdom generate disproportionately high visitor spending. International aviation networks play a critical role in sustaining these visitor flows, with major hub airports such as Singapore and Dubai acting as essential gateways between Australia and long-haul markets.

Purpose shapes how visitors spend and experience Australia

Not all tourists behave the same way once they arrive. Travel purpose strongly influences how long visitors stay, how much they spend, and which activities they participate in throughout their trip.

Section 4 — purpose & activities

Purpose & activities

Visitor behaviour differs substantially between domestic and international tourism markets. Domestic travel is heavily influenced by visiting friends and relatives, short leisure trips, and interstate holidays, while international tourism is more strongly associated with long-haul holidays, education, and business travel. These differences have major economic implications because some visitor types generate significantly higher spending than others. Beyond accommodation and transport, tourism spending is closely linked to dining, entertainment, retail, and nature-based experiences, highlighting the broad economic reach of Australia's visitor economy.

5
Recovery continues — but some markets remain far behind

Australia's tourism industry has recovered strongly since borders reopened, but the rebound has not occurred evenly across all visitor markets. Some countries have already surpassed pre-pandemic levels, while others remain well below their former peak.

Section 5 — timing & recovery

The uneven road back from COVID<

The COVID-19 pandemic reshaped Australia's tourism industry more dramatically than any previous disruption in modern history. International arrivals collapsed almost entirely during border closures, while domestic tourism fluctuated alongside lockdowns and travel restrictions. Although visitor activity has recovered rapidly since 2022, the rebound differs significantly between countries and Australian states. Markets such as India and the United States have returned strongly, while China continues to recover more slowly. These differences matter because the pace of international recovery will determine which destinations, airlines, and tourism sectors benefit most over the coming decade.
